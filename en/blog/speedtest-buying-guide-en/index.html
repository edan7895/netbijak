Broadband Speed Test Result

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

A broadband speed test measures your connection performance at a specific moment in time. When evaluating your results, it is important to remember that network conditions and hardware limitations can influence the outcome. Malaysian regulations suggest that a wired connection should typically reach 90% of your subscribed speed. If you are experiencing consistently slow speeds, check your router, cables, and device capabilities before assuming there is an issue with your ISP. Always use a wired Gigabit Ethernet connection for the most accurate diagnostic results. By eliminating these common variables, you can better determine if your broadband service is performing as it should.

How to Understand Your Broadband Speed Test Result

A broadband speed test shows how your internet connection is performing at the time of the test. To decide whether the result is good or unusually slow, compare it with your subscribed plan and make sure the test conditions are suitable.

What Speed Should You Get From Your Broadband Plan?

Your speed test will not always show exactly the speed stated on your broadband plan. Network conditions, equipment and the connection between your device and router can affect the result.

For a properly conducted wired test, a useful reference is 90% of the subscribed speed:

Broadband Plan90% Reference
100Mbps90Mbps
300Mbps270Mbps
500Mbps450Mbps
1Gbps900Mbps
2Gbps1.8Gbps

The 90% figure is based on Malaysia's broadband quality-of-service requirements. It should be used as a reference when assessing a connection, not as a guarantee that every speed test will produce the same number.

Source: Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC)

Why Is My Speed Test Lower Than My Broadband Plan?

A slower result does not automatically mean there is a problem with your ISP.

The test can be affected by:

  1. WiFi: Distance from the router, interference, wireless congestion and device capability can reduce speed.
  2. Router: Older routers or ports that only support 100Mbps can limit faster broadband plans.
  3. Ethernet cable: The cable must support the speed you are testing.
  4. Computer or phone: The device's network adapter and processing capability can affect the result.
  5. Other network activity: Downloads, cloud backups, streaming and other connected devices can use part of your available bandwidth.
  6. Test server: Results can vary depending on the server selected and current network conditions.

For a fibre connection, use a computer connected directly to the router by Gigabit Ethernet when you want to determine whether the broadband line itself is performing properly.

Why Is My 300Mbps Plan Only Getting Around 90Mbps?

A result consistently around 90–95Mbps on a 300Mbps, 500Mbps or faster plan is worth investigating.

One common cause is a 100Mbps hardware limitation somewhere between your computer and router. This can be caused by:

  1. A 100Mbps Ethernet port
  2. An older network adapter
  3. An unsuitable or damaged Ethernet cable
  4. A 100Mbps switch
  5. A router LAN port limited to 100Mbps

If the connection negotiates at only 100Mbps, changing your broadband package will not make the wired speed exceed that hardware limitation.

Check the Ethernet link speed on your computer and confirm that the router and network adapter support Gigabit Ethernet before reporting the result as an ISP speed problem.

Should You Test Broadband Speed Using WiFi or Ethernet?

Use Ethernet when you want to check whether your fibre connection is delivering the expected speed.

A WiFi speed test measures more than the broadband line. It also reflects the performance of the router, wireless technology, signal strength, interference and your device.

For example, a 500Mbps fibre connection may produce a lower result over WiFi while still performing normally over a wired Gigabit connection.

WiFi is useful for checking the performance you actually receive on your phone, laptop or other wireless devices. Ethernet is the better choice for diagnosing the broadband connection itself.

Mbps vs MB/s: Why Does My Download Speed Look Different?

Broadband plans are normally advertised in Mbps (megabits per second), while some download applications display MB/s (megabytes per second).

There are 8 bits in 1 byte, so:

  1. 100Mbps ≈ 12.5MB/s
  2. 300Mbps ≈ 37.5MB/s
  3. 500Mbps ≈ 62.5MB/s
  4. 1Gbps ≈ 125MB/s

These are theoretical conversions. Actual download speeds can be lower because of network overhead, the server's capacity and other factors.

If your 300Mbps broadband connection downloads a file at around 35MB/s, for example, that is not equivalent to only 35Mbps. It represents roughly 280Mbps before accounting for overhead.

When Should You Contact Your ISP?

Consider contacting your ISP when your speed remains significantly below the expected level after eliminating common equipment and testing issues.

Before reporting a slow connection:

  1. Connect a computer directly to the router using Gigabit Ethernet.
  2. Make sure the computer's network adapter and Ethernet cable support the subscribed speed.
  3. Stop large downloads, cloud backups and other heavy network activity.
  4. Run the speed test more than once.
  5. Try another suitable test server if available.
  6. Record the results and the time of testing.

If wired results remain consistently low under controlled conditions, the results are more useful when discussing the issue with your ISP.

A single low WiFi result is usually not enough to determine that the fibre connection itself is faulty.
