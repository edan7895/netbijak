Malaysia’s Internet Coverage Reaches 99.75% — So Why Can’t Everyone Get Fibre?

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

Malaysia’s national internet coverage in populated areas has reached an impressive 99.75%, yet many consumers still find themselves unable to access fibre broadband. This discrepancy exists because national coverage metrics include various technologies like 4G and 5G, which do not offer the same connectivity as fibre. Even within areas classified as having fibre infrastructure, factors like "premises passed" status, building-specific port availability, and individual ISP coverage determine whether a home can be connected. Simply living in a covered postcode does not guarantee service, as infrastructure often varies significantly between neighbouring buildings. Understanding these technical nuances is essential for consumers when choosing a provider. By focusing on address-level checks rather than national statistics, users can gain a clearer picture of their actual broadband options.

Malaysia’s internet and communications coverage in populated areas reached 99.75% as of July 31, 2026. But this does not mean 99.75% of Malaysian homes can immediately order fibre broadband. (BERNAMA)

The important distinction is between network coverage, premises passed and actual service availability.

What Does 99.75% Coverage Actually Mean?

The 99.75% figure is a national indicator for internet and communications coverage in populated areas.

It does not mean every house, condominium unit or commercial building has a fibre connection ready for installation.

A location can have strong mobile coverage while having limited fixed-line broadband options.

This is because Malaysia’s connectivity infrastructure includes different technologies:

  1. 4G / 5G — mobile network coverage
  2. Fibre — fixed broadband connectivity
  3. Other access technologies — used where fibre deployment is not practical or complete

So, “Internet coverage” does not automatically mean “fibre available at my address.”

NetBijak Hard Facts: What Does “Premises Passed” Mean?

Malaysia has reported more than 10.12 million fibre premises passed under the country’s broadband infrastructure rollout.

But what does “premises passed” actually mean?

It indicates that fibre infrastructure has been deployed to serve premises within the network footprint. It does not necessarily mean that fibre has already been connected inside your home, nor does it guarantee that an available fibre port is ready for immediate installation.

Think of it as three different stages:

Premises Passed → Fibre Availability → Available Port

A building can be within a fibre network footprint while an individual unit still requires installation. Availability can also depend on the specific ISP, building infrastructure and network capacity at that location.

That is why a postcode alone is not always enough to determine whether you can subscribe to a particular fibre plan.

Why Can My Neighbour Get Fibre But I Can’t?

Fibre availability can vary even between properties on the same street or within the same postcode.

Common reasons include:

  1. The fibre network does not serve your specific building
  2. A condominium’s internal fibre infrastructure is incomplete
  3. Your preferred ISP does not use the available infrastructure
  4. The building is covered by one provider but not another
  5. No suitable port is currently available
  6. Additional installation or building arrangements are required

This is especially common in high-rise buildings, where building-level infrastructure can be just as important as the surrounding fibre network.

99.75% Coverage Does Not Mean Every ISP Covers Your Home

The national coverage figure also does not tell you whether Unifi, Maxis, TIME, CelcomDigi or another provider is available at your exact premises.

Two buildings in the same postcode can have completely different broadband options.

For example, one condominium may have multiple fibre providers while another nearby building may have only one.

The practical sequence is:

Postcode → Street / Building → Premise → ISP availability → Available plan / port

This is why checking the actual location is more useful than relying on a national coverage percentage.

What Should You Check Before Choosing Fibre?

Before signing up for a broadband plan, check:

  1. Your exact address
  2. Street or building name
  3. Which ISPs actually serve the location
  4. Available broadband plans and speeds
  5. Whether installation or building approval is required
  6. Whether the provider can confirm an available connection

Why Does the 99.75% Figure Still Matter?

The figure is still significant because it shows how close Malaysia is to nationwide connectivity in populated areas. The government has stated that the next objective is to push coverage towards 100%. (The Star)

But for consumers, the more useful question is no longer simply:

“Does Malaysia have internet coverage?”

It is:

“Which broadband provider can actually connect my home?”

NetBijak’s Take

99.75% coverage is a national infrastructure milestone — not a guarantee that fibre is available at your front door.

For broadband users, the difference between coverage, premises passed and actual availability matters.

Before choosing a plan, check your actual location and the providers serving your building.

National coverage tells you how far the network has reached. Address-level availability tells you what you can actually buy.
