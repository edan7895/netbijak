CelcomDigi Fibre Merger: What Network Consolidation Actually Means for Fibre Users

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

The Celcom–Digi merger has led to a major consolidation of mobile infrastructure, but its impact on home fibre is significantly different. For fibre users, this integration does not mean two physical networks were simply joined, as CelcomDigi relies on a complex mix of its own assets and third-party partnerships like TIME. Customers should distinguish between backend billing system updates and actual changes to their fibre service. Importantly, internal billing migrations do not trigger new contracts or changes to your current internet speeds and fees. However, relocating your fibre connection or manually upgrading your plan may still lead to a fresh 24-month commitment. Ultimately, the merger is an administrative and mobile-focused evolution rather than an automatic reset of your existing home broadband terms.

The Celcom–Digi merger created CelcomDigi in November 2022, followed by a multi-year programme to integrate the two companies' networks and systems. But for home fibre users, “network consolidation” does not simply mean that two separate fibre networks were physically merged into one. CelcomDigi's public materials show a much more complicated mix of its own network assets and infrastructure partnerships. (CelcomDigi; CelcomDigi)

The merger mainly changed the network and systems behind the brand

CelcomDigi's major post-merger network integration programme has focused on consolidating and modernising the Celcom and Digi mobile networks. The company said the programme involved 18,000 sites and was designed to create a consolidated 4G/5G-ready network. By August 2026, CelcomDigi said all planned network integration and modernisation activities for 2026 had been completed, while final IT consolidation is targeted for the second half of 2027. (CelcomDigi)

That is different from saying:

Old Celcom Fibre + Old Digi Fibre = one new physical fibre network

For home broadband, CelcomDigi also uses third-party fibre infrastructure. In 2023, for example, CelcomDigi announced a partnership with TIME to use TIME's fibre infrastructure to extend CelcomDigi Fibre, particularly in condominiums and apartments. (CelcomDigi)

That means the physical fibre serving a CelcomDigi customer does not necessarily correspond to an old “Celcom network” or “Digi network”.

What happens to an existing CelcomDigi Fibre customer?

The current customer-facing changes are more straightforward.

In August 2026, CelcomDigi began separating certain previously combined billing accounts into individual accounts. CelcomDigi states that customers do not need to reapply or sign a new contract as a result of this exercise. Existing subscription plans, contract terms, monthly fees and internet speeds remain unchanged; only the billing arrangement changes. (CelcomDigi Help Centre)

So a billing-system migration should not be confused with a fibre-line migration.

Billing changes ≠ new fibre installation ≠ new contract.

What about package migration?

CelcomDigi can still change or migrate a customer's plan for reasons unrelated to the original Celcom–Digi network merger.

For example, customers moving to certain CelcomDigi One 2026 combinations may have their existing fibre plan changed to an eligible fibre plan, while the fibre and mobile contracts remain separate. (CelcomDigi Help Centre)

This distinction matters.

A customer should not assume:

“Celcom and Digi merged, therefore my Fibre contract has automatically restarted.”

There needs to be a specific plan change, relocation or contract event.

Relocation is different: a new 24-month contract can apply

CelcomDigi's current Fibre relocation policy is explicit.

If the new address has coverage and the relocation is completed, CelcomDigi charges RM200 for the relocation and starts a new 24-month contract after successful installation. (CelcomDigi Help Centre)

That is a genuine contract reset, but it is caused by relocation, not simply because Celcom and Digi merged.

The same process also shows why a coverage map is not the final answer: CelcomDigi says it will check port availability at the new address even when the area is within coverage. (CelcomDigi Help Centre)

Why coverage maps can overlap

CelcomDigi's current Fibre coverage checker allows users to identify whether an address is covered and even distinguishes between existing Celcom users, Digi users and new customers during the checking process. (CelcomDigi)

An overlapping map therefore does not automatically mean two separate fibre cables run through the same street.

The commercial coverage shown on a map can represent service availability rather than ownership of the physical last-mile infrastructure. CelcomDigi's partnership with TIME is a clear example of a retail fibre service being delivered using another operator's infrastructure. (CelcomDigi)

For the customer, the useful question is therefore not:

“Why do CelcomDigi and another provider both show coverage here?”

It is:

“Is there an available fibre port at my exact premises, and which infrastructure will serve it?”

What users should watch during the consolidation period

There are three situations worth checking carefully.

Existing Fibre customer receiving a system or billing change

Do not assume a new contract is required. CelcomDigi's August 2026 billing exercise specifically says existing plans, fees, speeds and contract terms remain unchanged. (CelcomDigi Help Centre)

Customer changing to another CelcomDigi package

Check whether the plan change creates a new commitment, removes rebates or changes the eligible Fibre plan. CelcomDigi's One 2026 documentation confirms that fibre and postpaid contracts can remain separate even when the package is changed. (CelcomDigi Help Centre)

Customer relocating

This is where a new 24-month Fibre contract can explicitly apply. (CelcomDigi Help Centre)

NetBijak's Take

The Celcom–Digi merger should not be treated as a simple “two fibre networks becoming one” story. The major physical consolidation was centred on the mobile network, while CelcomDigi Fibre can also ride on partner infrastructure such as TIME; for users, the important changes are specific billing, package and relocation events, not the merger headline itself.
