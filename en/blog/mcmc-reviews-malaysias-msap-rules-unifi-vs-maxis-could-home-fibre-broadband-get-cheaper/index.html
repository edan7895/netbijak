MCMC Reviews Malaysia’s MSAP Rules: Unifi vs Maxis — Could Home Fibre Broadband Get Cheaper?

MCMC Reviews MSAP: What Could It Mean for Malaysia’s Home Fibre Prices?

Malaysian broadband users could be heading towards another major change in the fibre broadband market as the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) moves towards a new review of the Mandatory Standard on Access Pricing (MSAP).

The MSAP is important because it regulates wholesale access pricing for certain telecommunications services. In the fibre broadband market, wholesale pricing can affect the costs faced by service providers that use another operator’s network infrastructure.

The current regulatory cycle follows the MSAP framework introduced in 2023. That framework reduced several regulated wholesale access prices, with the aim of encouraging more competitive broadband pricing and better value for consumers. (The Edge Malaysia⁠)

Why Does MSAP Matter to Home Broadband Users?

Most consumers do not see wholesale network charges directly on their monthly bill.

However, broadband providers may rely on wholesale network access when delivering fibre services. If wholesale costs become lower, providers may have more room to:

  1. Reduce monthly broadband prices
  2. Offer higher speeds at similar prices
  3. Introduce more promotional packages
  4. Improve network services
  5. Compete more aggressively for customers

The previous MSAP cycle provides an example of how regulatory changes can affect the retail market.

After the 2023 MSAP changes and subsequent commercial agreements, Maxis reduced the prices of several Home Fibre plans. At the time, Maxis offered 100Mbps at RM99/month, while higher-speed plans also became more competitively priced. (SoyaCincau⁠)

Unifi vs Maxis: Why Are They Important?

Telekom Malaysia’s Unifi and Maxis are two of the largest players in Malaysia’s fixed broadband market.

Unifi has a particularly important position because TM operates a large nationwide fibre infrastructure network. Other broadband providers have also used TM’s HSBB network to provide services to customers.

This means changes in wholesale access pricing can affect not only TM and Unifi, but also other providers that depend on regulated wholesale access.

The relationship between wholesale pricing and retail pricing is not always straightforward, however.

A lower wholesale price does not automatically mean a broadband provider must reduce its retail monthly price by the same amount.

Providers still need to consider network costs, customer acquisition, operations, equipment, promotions and profit margins.

Could the New MSAP Make Fibre Broadband Cheaper?

Possibly — but it is too early to guarantee a major retail price reduction.

Recent industry analysis suggests that the MSAP review could lead to lower wholesale broadband access prices, with implementation potentially taking place towards the end of 2026. However, analysts have also warned that providers may use some of the savings to improve margins rather than passing all of the savings directly to consumers. (MalaysianWireless⁠)

This means consumers could see several different outcomes.

Scenario 1: Lower Monthly Prices

Providers could reduce monthly fees to attract new customers.

Scenario 2: Same Price, Higher Speed

Instead of reducing prices, providers could offer faster speeds at similar monthly rates.

This has happened before in Malaysia’s broadband market, where regulatory changes were followed by faster broadband packages at more affordable price points. (Scribd⁠)

Scenario 3: More Promotions

Providers could compete through:

  1. Free installation
  2. Cashback
  3. Free routers
  4. Free months
  5. Higher-speed upgrades

rather than permanently reducing the standard monthly price.

What About Unifi and Maxis?

The MSAP review could create another competitive battle between major fibre providers.

Unifi has the advantage of TM’s extensive fibre infrastructure, while Maxis has historically competed through pricing, bundles and alternative network arrangements.

The previous MSAP cycle already demonstrated how changes in wholesale network costs can influence retail broadband competition. Maxis, for example, introduced significant Home Fibre price reductions after finalising its access agreement with TM. (The Edge Malaysia⁠)

However, consumers should not assume that the next MSAP automatically means Unifi or Maxis will cut prices.

The actual outcome will depend on the final MCMC framework, commercial agreements between providers and how each telco chooses to compete.

Should You Wait Before Signing Up for Fibre?

If you need broadband now, waiting solely because of the MSAP review may not necessarily be the best decision.

Broadband providers frequently launch promotions independently of regulatory changes.

Instead, compare the plans currently available and look at:

  1. Monthly price
  2. Internet speed
  3. Upload speed
  4. Contract period
  5. Installation fees
  6. Router or mesh equipment
  7. Promotions
  8. Your actual household usage

If a provider offers a strong promotion today, it could still be better value than waiting several months for a possible price reduction.

What Could Happen to Malaysian Fibre Prices Next?

The most interesting possibility is not necessarily a simple price war.

Malaysia could see a combination of:

Lower prices + faster speeds + better promotions.

The previous MSAP cycle showed that regulatory changes can influence broadband competition, but the final benefits depend on how providers respond.

For consumers, the biggest advantage is having more choices.
