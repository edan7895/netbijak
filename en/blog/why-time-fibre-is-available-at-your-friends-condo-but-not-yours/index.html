Why TIME Fibre Is Available at Your Friend’s Condo But Not Yours

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

Many residents are confused why TIME Fibre is available in some condominiums but not others nearby. Unlike mobile coverage that serves a wide area, TIME operates its own private fibre network that must be physically installed in each individual building. Whether a building has service depends on whether it has been connected to TIME’s infrastructure and if the internal wiring supports their technology. Factors like population density and installation costs often dictate where TIME prioritizes its network expansion. Even if your building has other fibre providers, it does not guarantee that TIME has the necessary infrastructure in place to offer its service. If your condo is not currently covered, you can use the official coverage checker to add your address to their wish list for future network expansions.

Why Does Your Friend Have TIME Fibre When You Don’t?

You may have experienced this before.

Your friend lives in a condominium just a few kilometres away. They are using TIME Fibre, paying a reasonable monthly fee and enjoying fast internet.

You check TIME’s website, enter your own address — and suddenly:

TIME Fibre is not available at your address.

So why can one condo get TIME Fibre while another nearby building cannot?

The short answer is: fibre broadband availability is often determined at building level, not simply by the general area.

TIME itself states that plan availability varies by building, and its coverage checker asks customers to enter their building or street name to determine whether the service is available. (Time Internet)

Here’s why.

1. Fibre Coverage Is Not the Same as Mobile Coverage

One common misunderstanding is assuming that if TIME has coverage in your neighbourhood, every building should be able to subscribe.

Fibre broadband does not work that way.

For mobile networks, a nearby base station can potentially serve many surrounding locations.

Fibre broadband requires a physical network connection to reach the building and, ultimately, the customer’s unit.

TIME operates its own fibre network rather than simply using TM’s nationwide fibre network. TIME has previously explained that it chose to build and operate its own network to have greater control over service quality. (Time Internet)

That means TIME has to physically deploy its network where it wants to offer service.

2. Your Friend’s Condo May Already Be a “TIME-Ready” Building

This is probably the biggest reason.

Imagine two condominiums:

Condo A

  1. TIME fibre infrastructure has already been deployed
  2. The building is connected to TIME’s network
  3. Fibre can be connected to individual units
  4. Residents can subscribe

Condo B

  1. TIME’s network may be nearby
  2. But the building itself is not connected or not yet ready
  3. Residents cannot simply order TIME and have the service activated

So even if the two buildings are only 500 metres apart, the result can be completely different.

TIME’s own FAQ states that residential plan availability is subject to the customer’s address and that availability varies by building. (Time Internet)

3. Condo Management and Building Infrastructure Matter

High-rise buildings are different from landed houses.

A fibre provider may need to bring fibre into the building, route cables through common areas and connect the network to the relevant telecommunications infrastructure.

For an existing condominium, the available risers, ducts, telecom rooms and internal cabling can affect how easily a new provider can deploy its service.

TIME’s installation FAQ also distinguishes between pre-laid units, where a fibre socket is already available, and non-prelaid units, where installers need to pull fibre from outside the unit into the home. (Time Internet)

This is why two condos in the same neighbourhood can have completely different installation situations.

4. TIME May Have Prioritised Certain Buildings First

Fibre deployment is expensive.

A provider has to consider factors such as:

  1. Number of homes in a building
  2. Population density
  3. Expected customer demand
  4. Existing infrastructure
  5. Deployment cost
  6. Network expansion priorities

A large condominium with hundreds of units can potentially be more attractive for fibre deployment than a smaller building with fewer potential customers.

TIME has previously explained that its deployment strategy prioritised urban areas, where higher population density can make fibre rollout more cost-effective. It also noted that connecting a high-rise building with hundreds of units can be more economically attractive than extending fibre to scattered homes. (Time Internet)

This helps explain why TIME may appear to be “everywhere” in one neighbourhood while still being unavailable in another building.

5. Being in the Same City Does Not Mean Having the Same Fibre Options

This is especially common in areas with many condominiums.

For example, imagine:

LocationTIME Fibre
Condo A Available
Condo B Not available
Condo C Available
Condo D Not available

All four buildings could be located along the same road.

There is nothing unusual about this.

The important question is not:

“Does TIME cover my neighbourhood?”

The better question is:

“Is TIME Fibre available at my exact building?”

That is also why TIME provides an address/building-level coverage checker rather than simply publishing a broad city-level coverage map. (Time Internet)

6. Your Condo May Have Fibre, But Not TIME Fibre

Another common misunderstanding is:

“My condo already has fibre, so TIME should be able to use it.”

Not necessarily.

A building can have fibre infrastructure from one provider without automatically being connected to another provider’s network.

TIME has its own fibre network infrastructure, while other providers may use different infrastructure arrangements.

So your condo having Unifi, Maxis or another fibre service does not automatically mean TIME can activate a line there.

TIME has stated that it owns and operates its own fibre network, while other providers may have different network arrangements. (Time Internet)

7. “Not Available” Does Not Always Mean “Never Available”

If TIME is unavailable at your address today, it does not necessarily mean it will remain unavailable permanently.

TIME’s coverage pages allow customers in uncovered areas to add their address to its Wish List, and TIME says it is continuing to expand its coverage. (Time Internet)

This is particularly relevant if you live in a growing residential development.

A building that is not covered today could potentially become covered after network expansion.

However, there is no guarantee of a specific rollout date unless TIME confirms it.

8. What Should You Do If Your Condo Doesn’t Have TIME?

Before deciding that TIME is impossible for your building, check the address directly.

Step 1: Check your exact building

Use TIME’s official coverage checker and enter your building or street name. (Time Internet)

Step 2: Check other fibre providers

If TIME is unavailable, compare the alternatives available at your address.

Don’t choose a provider simply because your friend has it.

Step 3: Ask your condo management

If you know other residents are interested in TIME, you can ask your Joint Management Body (JMB), Management Corporation (MC) or building management whether TIME has previously approached the building or whether there are plans for additional fibre infrastructure.

Step 4: Add your address to TIME’s Wish List

If TIME is unavailable, TIME provides an option to submit your address to its Wish List. (Time Internet)

TIME Fibre Availability Can Be Building-Specific

The biggest lesson is simple:

Living near a TIME customer does not mean you can automatically subscribe to TIME.

Your friend may have TIME because their condominium is already connected to TIME’s fibre network, while your building may not yet have the necessary infrastructure.

This is why checking broadband availability by exact address or building is much more useful than simply asking which fibre providers operate in your city.

NetBijak Tips

Don’t choose a broadband plan based on your friend’s address.

The same neighbourhood can have very different broadband options from one building to another.

Before signing up, always:

  1. Check your exact building.
  2. Compare all available providers.
  3. Look at monthly price and contract length.
  4. Check download and upload speeds.
  5. Consider how many people will use the connection.
  6. Check whether the building has existing fibre infrastructure.
  7. Don’t assume that because one provider is available, every provider will be available.

The best broadband plan is not necessarily the fastest one — it is the best plan actually available at your address.
