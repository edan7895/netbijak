TIME Broadband in Klang Valley Condos: Is It Always the Fastest?

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

TIME Broadband has long been a go-to choice for Klang Valley condo residents due to its early focus on high-rise infrastructure. However, widespread availability does not automatically make it the fastest or most suitable option for every building. While TIME was once synonymous with symmetrical upload and download speeds, many of its current plans now feature higher download-to-upload ratios. Choosing the best provider depends entirely on what infrastructure is actually installed at your specific building rather than general city-wide coverage claims. Residents should prioritize checking availability for their specific unit and understanding their own needs, such as whether they require high upload capacity for professional or personal use. If TIME is unavailable, other providers like Unifi or Maxis, or even 5G home internet, may serve as viable alternatives.

For condo residents in Kuala Lumpur and Selangor, TIME is often one of the first fibre providers to check.

There is a reason for that. TIME built its residential network around high-rise properties before expanding further into landed homes, giving it a long-established presence in the Klang Valley condo market. TIME confirms that its fibre service started with high-rises.

But being widely available in condos does not mean TIME is automatically the fastest option.

For condo broadband, the building matters more than the postcode.

Why TIME Has a Strong Condo Presence

TIME's high-rise focus goes back more than a decade. Its 2018 annual report said high-rise homes and commercial buildings remained a key focus, with network expansion continuing across the Klang Valley. TIME Annual Report 2018.

That strategy helps explain why TIME is so familiar to condo residents today.

TIME has also made its fibre infrastructure available to other providers. In 2023, CelcomDigi announced that it would use TIME's network to expand fibre access, specifically highlighting condominiums and apartments as part of the collaboration. CelcomDigi and TIME's fibre partnership also highlighted TIME's then-symmetric upload and download offering.

So TIME's strong presence in Klang Valley condos is more about network footprint and deployment history than proof that condo residents universally prefer TIME.

TIME's Upload-Speed Reputation

TIME also became known for something that mattered to heavier upload users: symmetrical fibre speeds.

In a symmetrical connection, the advertised upload speed matches the download speed. That was a notable feature of TIME's fibre offering and was specifically highlighted by CelcomDigi when it announced its 2023 partnership with TIME. CelcomDigi and TIME.

However, that reputation needs a 2026 update.

TIME's current residential plans are:

TIME currently lists 200Mbps as 200/200Mbps. The 600Mbps, 1Gbps and 2Gbps plans are 600/500Mbps, 1Gbps/500Mbps and 2Gbps/500Mbps respectively. TIME Home Fibre plans show the current download and upload specifications.

So the statement “TIME is symmetrical” is no longer accurate across its entire home range.

The more useful question is whether you actually need high upload capacity.

For someone mainly streaming Netflix, browsing and downloading games, download speed is usually the bigger consideration.

For a household regularly uploading large videos, running cloud backups, accessing a NAS remotely or sending CCTV footage to remote storage, upload speed becomes much more relevant.

Is TIME Always the Fastest in a Condo?

No single provider can be called the fastest for every condominium.

TIME itself states that plan availability varies by building. Its current 2Gbps service is also limited to selected locations. TIME Home Internet FAQ.

That creates a practical difference between two condos in the same neighbourhood.

One building may have TIME 2Gbps.

Another may only offer lower-speed TIME plans.

A nearby building may have no TIME fibre at all.

Look at the building, not the postcode.

A city-wide coverage claim is not enough for a condo resident. The relevant question is what service is actually available at your building and your unit.

What About Older Condos?

An old condominium does not automatically mean you cannot install TIME.

The key distinction is whether the building already has Pre-laid Fibre.

With a pre-laid fibre installation, the unit already has a fibre socket, making the final connection relatively straightforward.

With Non-prelaid Fibre, TIME can bring fibre into the unit as part of the installation. Its current installation information provides up to 20 metres of basic cabling for a high-rise building without pre-laid fibre, measured from the point of entry to the equipment location. TIME installation information.

TIME's installation documentation also describes pulling fibre from the nearest node at the riser or MDF into the unit for non-prelaid high-rise buildings. TIME Service Schedule C.

In practical terms, that can mean bringing the fibre from the building's point of entry, riser or Main Distribution Frame (MDF) into the apartment and connecting it to the Optical Network Unit (ONU).

The absence of a fibre socket inside the apartment therefore does not automatically mean TIME is unavailable.

The bigger question is whether TIME's network has reached the building and whether the building's internal infrastructure allows the installation.

When TIME Really May Not Be an Option

An older condo is more likely to become a problem when the issue is with the building itself rather than the age of the property.

For example, TIME may not yet serve the building, the building may not have the required fibre infrastructure, or additional installation work may be required.

TIME's current rate card lists 20 metres of standard fibre cabling from the point of entry for a high-rise building without pre-laid fibre. It also lists separate charges for certain additional work, including installing new fibre from a riser room to the point of entry. TIME Installation Rate Card.

So there are really two separate questions:

Can TIME reach the building?

and

Can TIME's fibre be brought from the building into your unit?

Do not confuse the two.

If no fixed fibre service is available, 5G Home Internet may be another option. But that is a different type of connection and should be assessed based on actual indoor 5G signal and network performance at the property.

What Should Condo Residents Compare?

Don't compare broadband providers based only on their reputation across Kuala Lumpur or Selangor.

Check what you can actually get at your address:

Download and upload speed — especially if you upload large amounts of data.

Available plan tiers — your building may not offer the provider's full product range.

Installation conditions — particularly in older high-rise buildings.

Alternative fibre providers — compare the services available in the same building rather than assuming one provider is better everywhere.
