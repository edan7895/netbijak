What Broadband Speed Do You Need for Netflix 4K?

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

Streaming Netflix in 4K requires a stable internet speed of at least 15Mbps, making a standard 100Mbps fibre plan more than sufficient for most single-stream households. While 100Mbps is enough for one 4K stream, higher speeds like 300Mbps are better suited for homes with multiple simultaneous users and heavy background activities like gaming or video conferencing. It is important to remember that upgrading your broadband plan does not improve picture quality beyond Netflix's requirements; rather, it provides extra capacity to prevent slowdowns during peak usage. If you experience buffering despite having a fast connection, the issue likely stems from Wi-Fi signal strength, router placement, or hardware limitations rather than a lack of bandwidth. Ultimately, you should choose a plan based on the total number of connected devices and concurrent users in your home rather than just your desire for 4K streaming.

Watching Netflix in 4K sounds like something that requires a very fast broadband connection.

You might assume that you need:

300Mbps, 500Mbps or even 1Gbps

to enjoy 4K streaming.

But the reality is quite different.

According to Netflix's current official recommendation, 4K Ultra HD streaming requires a stable internet connection of at least 15Mbps.

That means a 100Mbps fibre plan has considerably more bandwidth than Netflix's minimum recommended speed for a single 4K stream.

So the more useful question is not:

“Do I need 300Mbps for Netflix 4K?”

It is:

“How many 4K streams and other internet activities will happen at the same time?”

How Much Internet Speed Does Netflix 4K Need?

Netflix currently recommends:

Netflix QualityResolutionRecommended Speed
HD720p3Mbps+
Full HD1080p5Mbps+
Ultra HD4K15Mbps+

These are Netflix's recommended connection speeds for the relevant video quality.

So, for one Netflix 4K stream, 15Mbps or more is the official recommendation.

However, this does not mean that subscribing to a 15Mbps broadband plan is necessarily the best choice for your household.

Your broadband connection is shared with everything else happening in your home.

Is 100Mbps Enough for Netflix 4K?

Yes, for most households, 100Mbps is more than enough for a single 4K Netflix stream.

In fact, 100Mbps provides more than six times Netflix's recommended 15Mbps connection speed for 4K.

For example:

Netflix 4K → ~15Mbps recommended
Broadband → 100Mbps

That leaves considerably more bandwidth for other activities.

A household could potentially have Netflix running while other users browse the web, use social media, attend video meetings or perform other online activities.

Of course, actual performance depends on the quality and stability of the connection and how much bandwidth other activities consume.

Unifi currently positions its 100Mbps plan for small households and apartments with around 2–4 users/devices, while its 300Mbps plan is positioned for families and home offices with around 4–6 users/devices.

These are provider recommendations rather than technical limits.

What If You Have Multiple 4K TVs?

This is where the required bandwidth starts to change.

If one 4K Netflix stream requires around 15Mbps according to Netflix's recommendation, then several simultaneous streams require more overall capacity.

For example:

UsageApproximate Recommended Netflix Bandwidth
1 × 4K stream15Mbps+
2 × 4K streams30Mbps+
3 × 4K streams45Mbps+
4 × 4K streams60Mbps+

These figures are simple calculations based on Netflix's 15Mbps recommendation per 4K stream. They are not a guarantee of actual performance.

You also need to leave room for:

  1. Video calls
  2. Gaming
  3. Downloads
  4. Cloud backups
  5. Software updates
  6. Smartphones
  7. Smart TVs
  8. CCTV
  9. Other connected devices

This is why a household may reasonably choose 100Mbps or 300Mbps even though one Netflix 4K stream only needs 15Mbps.

Does 300Mbps Make Netflix 4K Better?

Not necessarily.

If your internet connection is already stable and capable of delivering Netflix 4K, upgrading from 100Mbps to 300Mbps does not make the picture quality three times better.

Netflix's own guidance says that it adapts streaming quality according to the available internet connection and may initially play at a lower resolution before switching to a higher resolution.

The extra bandwidth from a 300Mbps plan is more useful when other activities are happening at the same time.

For example:

TV 1 → Netflix 4K
TV 2 → Netflix 4K
PC → Downloading a 100GB game
Parent → Video conference
Phones → Social media

In this situation, 300Mbps provides more overall capacity.

How Much Data Does Netflix 4K Use?

Speed isn't the only thing to consider.

Netflix currently states that Ultra HD (4K) can use up to 7GB of data per hour per device under its High data-usage setting.

For a four-hour movie session:

7GB × 4 hours = up to 28GB

And a household watching 4K Netflix regularly can consume a significant amount of data.

For unlimited fibre broadband plans, this is generally less of a concern than for capped connections.

Still, it's useful to understand the difference between:

Bandwidth → how much data can be transferred at a given time

and

Data usage → how much data you consume over time.

They are not the same thing.

Why Does Netflix 4K Sometimes Buffer on Fast Broadband?

If you have a 300Mbps or 500Mbps plan but Netflix still buffers, the problem may not be your broadband package.

Netflix notes that many factors can affect streaming quality and recommends checking the network connection.

Possible causes include:

1. Weak Wi-Fi

Your broadband may be fast, but the TV could have a weak Wi-Fi connection.

2. Router Location

A TV located far from the router, especially with several walls in between, may experience weaker performance.

3. Network Congestion

Other devices in your home may be consuming significant bandwidth.

4. Device Limitations

Your TV or streaming device needs to support the required 4K capabilities.

Netflix notes that the devices and connections in the setup need to support the desired quality.

5. Netflix Plan

Your Netflix subscription also needs to support the desired video quality.

Does Your TV Need to Support 4K?

Yes.

Having a fast broadband plan does not automatically give you 4K video.

Your setup needs to support it.

Netflix explains that for 4K or HDR, the relevant devices, ports and cables also need to support the required specifications.

For example, if you're using:

Broadband → Wi-Fi Router → Streaming Device → AV Receiver → TV

a limitation anywhere in the setup could affect the final video quality.

So upgrading from 100Mbps to 1Gbps will not turn a non-4K television into a 4K television.

100Mbps vs 300Mbps for Netflix 4K

So which one should you choose?

100Mbps

A good option to consider if:

  1. You have one or two TVs
  2. Usually only one 4K stream is active
  3. Household internet usage is moderate
  4. You don't frequently download huge files
  5. You want to keep your monthly broadband cost lower

300Mbps

Worth considering if:

  1. Several people use the internet simultaneously
  2. You have multiple TVs
  3. Multiple 4K streams may run at once
  4. Family members game while others stream
  5. Large downloads happen regularly
  6. Several people work or study from home

The key difference is overall household capacity, not Netflix picture quality.

What About 500Mbps or 1Gbps?

For Netflix alone, you generally don't need 500Mbps or 1Gbps just to watch 4K.

Higher-speed plans make more sense when Netflix is only one part of a much heavier household internet workload.

For example:

4K Netflix
  1. Multiple 4K TVs
  2. Gaming
  3. Large downloads
  4. WFH
  5. Cloud backup

In that situation, a faster broadband plan may provide more headroom.

But if the main activity is simply:

One TV watching Netflix 4K

then paying significantly more for a 1Gbps plan may not provide a noticeable improvement in Netflix picture quality.

NetBijak's Take

Netflix currently recommends a stable 15Mbps or higher connection for 4K Ultra HD.

The reason someone might choose 300Mbps, 500Mbps or 1Gbps is usually not because Netflix 4K requires it.

It is because the household may have several activities happening simultaneously.

For example:

Netflix 4K + Gaming + WFH + Downloads + Multiple Users

In that situation, additional bandwidth can make sense.

Our view is simple:

Don't buy a faster broadband plan just because you see “4K”.

Instead, consider:

  1. How many people use your internet
  2. How many 4K streams you watch simultaneously
  3. Whether you game
  4. Whether anyone works from home
  5. Whether you download large files
  6. Your Wi-Fi coverage
  7. The total monthly cost

For one or two moderate users, 100Mbps may already be more than enough.
