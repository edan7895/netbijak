Home Fibre vs Business Fibre: Why Commercial Plans Can Cost 3x More

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

Business fibre plans often come with a significant price premium, but this is rarely due to the speed of the fibre connection itself. Instead, the extra cost covers essential business-grade features like Fixed IP addresses, backup connectivity, and faster support response times. While residential fibre is designed for basic internet access, business plans are built to minimize operational downtime, which can be costly for a company. Not all business fibre is created equal, and it is important to distinguish between standard business broadband and dedicated internet access with guaranteed performance targets. By understanding these differences, business owners can make informed decisions about which connectivity package truly suits their operational needs.

A business may pay far more for an internet connection that does not look dramatically faster than a home fibre plan.

The reason is that business internet is not priced on Mbps alone.

Home Fibre is mainly about getting an internet connection into your house. Business Fibre can add Fixed IP, backup connectivity, business support and service commitments. At the higher end, Dedicated Internet can add dedicated bandwidth, symmetrical speeds and defined performance targets.

That is where the bigger price difference comes from.

Business Fibre Does Not Automatically Cost 3x More

The “3x” figure is not a standard industry markup.

For example, TIME currently lists Home Fibre 1Gbps at RM199/month, while its Business Fibre 1Gbps is RM258/month. A Fixed IP can then be added for RM200/month, bringing that combination to RM458/month. TIME Home Fibre TIME Business Fibre

So the jump is not simply:

Home 1Gbps → Business 1Gbps = 3x the price.

The bigger difference appears when a business starts paying for features that a residential customer usually does not need.

Fixed IP Is About Access, Not Speed

One of the clearest examples is a Fixed IP.

A normal home user can usually operate perfectly well without one. A business may need a consistent public IP for VPN access, IP allowlisting, remote access or hosting.

TIME currently offers Fixed IP as an add-on at RM200/month and allows up to five Fixed IPs on a Business Fibre line. TIME Business Fixed IP

Unifi Business also lists Fixed IP as an optional add-on from RM200, while CelcomDigi Business and Maxis Business also offer Fixed IP options on their business fibre services. Unifi Business Fibre CelcomDigi Business Fibre Maxis Business Fibre

The important point is that Fixed IP does not make the fibre line faster.

It gives the business a stable public network identity that can be useful for systems that need to recognise or reach that connection.

Business Fibre Is Not Always Symmetrical

Another common assumption is that business broadband automatically means equal upload and download speeds.

It does not.

TIME's current Business Fibre plans are listed at 300/300Mbps, 600/500Mbps, 1Gbps/500Mbps and 2Gbps/1Gbps. TIME Business Fibre plans

So even within the same provider, Business Fibre does not necessarily mean 1:1 upload and download.

The same distinction matters across other operators. Business plans should be checked by their actual upload specification rather than by the word “Business” on the package.

Where symmetrical bandwidth becomes much more explicit is the Dedicated Internet category.

TIME's Secured Dedicated Internet service advertises dedicated internet access with a 1:1 contention ratio, symmetrical upload and download speeds, and guaranteed bi-directional bandwidth. TIME Secured Dedicated Internet

That is a different product from ordinary Business Fibre.

SLA Is Where the Business Premium Starts to Make Sense

For a company, the important question is not only:

“How fast is the connection?”

It is also:

“What happens when it fails?”

That is where an SLA (Service Level Agreement) or defined service target becomes important.

A business-oriented service may specify targets around availability, response time or restoration.

For example, TIME's Secured Dedicated Internet currently advertises:

Up to 99.9% uptime

Mean Time to Respond within 15 minutes

Mean Time to Repair within 4 hours

TIME Secured Dedicated Internet

Unifi Business, at the broadband level, currently advertises a 24-hour restoration target for its Business Broadband plans, rather than the 4-hour MTTR advertised by TIME's Dedicated Internet service. Unifi Business Fibre

This is why it is dangerous to say:

“Business Fibre comes with a 4-hour SLA.”

That is not a universal feature. The four-hour metric is tied to TIME's Dedicated Internet service in the material cited above, not to every Business Fibre plan in Malaysia.

Why Does a 4-Hour Repair Target Matter?

Because business downtime has a financial cost.

Imagine a retail outlet losing its internet connection.

The outage may affect the payment terminal, cloud POS, VoIP, CCTV access, cloud accounting or office applications.

For a household, four hours without internet is an inconvenience.

For a business, four hours can mean lost transactions, staff downtime or disrupted operations.

That changes the calculation.

A company is not necessarily paying extra because the fibre itself is somehow “better fibre”.

It may be paying to reduce the cost and uncertainty of downtime.

Backup Internet Is Another Part of the Price

Business plans may also include a secondary connection so the company does not immediately go offline when the primary fibre fails.

Maxis Business Fibre currently includes an always-on 4G wireless backup service, while CelcomDigi Business Fibre also markets wireless backup as part of its business connectivity offering. Maxis Business Fibre CelcomDigi Business Fibre

Unifi Business also offers Biz 5G Wireless Backup as an add-on, with the 1Gbps and 2Gbps plans currently showing it as included. Unifi Business Fibre

This creates an important distinction:

Backup Internet is not the same as guaranteed primary-line performance.

It is there to keep essential connectivity available while the main service is being restored.

Maxis' business terms make this especially clear: its wireless backup provides temporary internet while the primary service is being restored, but certain services such as VPN may not work through the backup connection, and the backup itself is provided on a best-effort basis without service-level assurance. Maxis Business Fibre Terms

That is a useful detail for businesses that depend on VPN or Fixed IP.

Business Fibre and Dedicated Internet Are Not the Same Thing

This is the biggest distinction to understand.

Home Fibre

Designed for residential use.

You are mainly paying for:

Internet access + bandwidth + normal support

Business Fibre

Adds features designed around business operations.

Depending on the provider, this can include:

Fixed IP

Wireless Backup

Business Voice

Business-oriented support

Business hardware

Unifi, Maxis, CelcomDigi and TIME all currently offer business fibre products with different combinations of these features. Unifi Business Fibre Maxis Business Fibre CelcomDigi Business Fibre TIME Business Fibre

Dedicated Internet

This is where the service moves beyond ordinary shared broadband.

TIME's Dedicated Internet offering explicitly includes 1:1 contention, symmetrical bandwidth, guaranteed bandwidth and defined service targets. TIME Secured Dedicated Internet

So when a dedicated business connection costs dramatically more, the customer is paying for a different level of network service — not simply a larger number beside “Mbps”.

Who Actually Needs Business Fibre?

Not every company needs enterprise-grade connectivity.

Business Fibre becomes more relevant when the connection supports something operationally important:

Retail and POS

An internet outage can interrupt transactions.

Small offices

Staff may rely heavily on cloud apps, VoIP and file sharing.

Remote access

A Fixed IP may be needed for VPN or IP allowlisting.

Cloud-heavy businesses

Upload capacity can matter as much as download.

Businesses with low tolerance for downtime

Backup connectivity and defined service targets can have a direct operational value.

A home user who browses, streams, games and occasionally works remotely may gain little from paying for these features.

The Real Reason Commercial Internet Costs More

The biggest price difference is rarely explained by Mbps alone.

You may be paying for:

Fixed IP

Backup connectivity

Business support

Voice services

Dedicated bandwidth

Symmetrical connectivity

Defined response or restoration targets

So the better question is not:

“Why is Business Fibre so expensive?”

It is:

“Which business problem is the extra money solving?”

For a company that only needs internet access, a residential plan may be enough.

For a company that needs a fixed public IP, backup access and predictable fault handling, the business premium becomes easier to understand.


NetBijak's Take

Business internet is not simply Home Fibre at a higher price.

The premium can buy Fixed IP, backup connectivity, business support and defined service targets. At the Dedicated Internet level, it can also buy dedicated bandwidth and stronger performance commitments.

Before paying two or three times more, identify the business problem you are actually paying to solve.
