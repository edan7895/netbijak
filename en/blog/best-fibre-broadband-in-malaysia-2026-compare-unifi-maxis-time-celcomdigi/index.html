Best Fibre Broadband in Malaysia 2026: Compare Unifi, Maxis, TIME & CelcomDigi

Which fibre broadband is actually the best in Malaysia in 2026?

If you ask ten different people in Klang Valley, Penang, or Johor Bahru, you will get ten different answers. A resident living in a high-rise condo in Mont Kiara will likely praise TIME for its symmetrical gigabit speeds at rock-bottom prices. A family living in a landed house in Ipoh will probably say Unifi is the only reliable choice because Telekom Malaysia (TM) owns the physical fibre cable running down their street. Meanwhile, mobile power users might swear by Maxis or CelcomDigi due to heavily discounted mobile-broadband bundle rates.

Here is the truth: there is no single best broadband provider for everyone in Malaysia.

The "best" internet plan for your home depends entirely on eight real-world factors:

  1. Your exact physical address (down to the building block or street name)
  2. Property type (Landed house vs. High-rise condo/apartment)
  3. Number of active users and connected smart devices
  4. Primary household usage (4K streaming, WFH video calls, low-latency gaming)
  5. Monthly budget
  6. Infrastructure availability (Direct 100% fibre vs. HSBA wholesale network)
  7. Contract length flexibility (Standard 24-month vs. short-term or no-contract options)
  8. Current promotional offers and mobile line bundling discounts

At NetBijak, our goal is transparent, unbiased clarity. We break down the Malaysian home broadband market so you can select the exact plan you need—without overpaying for unused speed or getting trapped in an unfavorable contract.

Which is the Best Fibre Broadband in Malaysia?

Because network quality and pricing vary based on your setup, here are our category recommendations:

  1. Best for Widest Availability: Unifi. If you live in a landed terrace house, semi-D, bungalow, or outside major urban centers, Telekom Malaysia's Unifi network is often the primary (or only) physical fibre infrastructure available.
  2. Best for High-Rise & Condo Living: TIME Dotcom. If your condo or apartment building is within TIME's coverage footprint, it delivers the highest speed per Ringgit, excellent Wi-Fi 6/7 routers, and balanced upload speeds.
  3. Best Value for Money (Standalone Plan): TIME 200Mbps (RM99/mth) or Unifi 100Mbps (RM89/mth). For under RM100 a month, these plans easily support a typical 3-to-5-person household.
  4. Best Value for Integrated Mobile Bundles: CelcomDigi Fibre or Maxis Family Plan. Combining your home internet with your mobile lines can yield RM20–RM50 in monthly savings across your total household telco bill.
  5. Best for Competitive Gamers: TIME Fibre. TIME’s 100% direct optical infrastructure consistently provides lower latency (ping) to regional gaming servers (Singapore, Hong Kong) and fast upload speeds for live streaming.
  6. Best for Remote Workers (WFH): TIME or Unifi 300Mbps+. High upload capacity ensures video calls (Zoom, Teams) remain crisp while syncing large cloud backups in the background.

Provider Deep Dives

1. Unifi (Telekom Malaysia)

Unifi remains the backbone of Malaysia’s fixed broadband landscape. Operating on TM's extensive nationwide optical network, Unifi provides service directly or via wholesale access (HSBA) to nearly every other fibre provider in the country.

Strengths

  1. Unmatched Reach: Available in over 95% of populated areas across Peninsular Malaysia, Sabah, and Sarawak.
  2. Service Restoration Guarantee: TM provides a 24-hour service restoration guarantee for home fibre customers.
  3. Entertainment Bundles: Options to bundle Unifi TV, Netflix, Disney+ Hotstar, or HBO Max directly into the bill.

Weaknesses

  1. Lower Upload Speeds on Base Plans: Download speeds are high, but entry-level plans often feature asymmetrical upload speeds (e.g., 300Mbps download with 50Mbps upload).
  2. Higher Standard Pricing: Standard retail pricing for standalone plans is slightly higher compared to niche competitors like TIME.
  3. Who should choose Unifi: Landed house owners, suburban/rural residents, and households seeking a reliable, widely supported home connection.
  4. Who should avoid it: High-rise dwellers who have active TIME coverage and want symmetrical upload speeds at lower rates.

2. Maxis Home Fibre

Maxis offers home fibre internet primarily by utilizing TM’s HSBA network infrastructure, alongside its own limited direct fibre rollouts in select developments.

Strengths

  1. Integrated Maxis Family Bundles: Exceptional value if your household uses Maxis Postpaid mobile lines. Combined plans (like the Maxis Family Plan) offer unlimited mobile lines alongside 500Mbps or 1Gbps home fibre.
  2. 4G/5G Backup Router Options: Certain business and premium home tiers offer automatic 4G/5G mobile data failover if the physical fibre line suffers an outage.
  3. Wi-Fi 6 Routers Included: All standard 24-month contracts come with modern Wi-Fi 6 router hardware.

Weaknesses

  1. Fewer Standalone Advantages: If you do not use Maxis mobile service, the standalone fibre packages are virtually identical in price and performance to Unifi.
  2. Who should choose Maxis: Existing Maxis postpaid subscribers looking to consolidate mobile and home internet bills into a single discounted package.
  3. Who should avoid it: Non-Maxis mobile users who can secure cheaper standalone rates elsewhere.

3. TIME Fibre Broadband

TIME Dotcom operates its own 10G-PON full-fibre network, focusing primarily on high-density urban areas, commercial towers, and high-rise condominiums.

Strengths

  1. Symmetrical & High Upload Speeds: Entry plans offer 1:1 download-to-upload ratios (e.g., 200Mbps download / 200Mbps upload), making it top-tier for gaming, content creation, and cloud backups.
  2. Superior Price-to-Speed Ratio: Offers 200Mbps for RM99/month and up to 1Gbps for RM199/month.
  3. Flexible Contracts: Offers standard 24-month plans, 12-month plans, or No-Contract options (with upfront installation fee).
  4. Cutting-Edge Routers: Higher-tier plans include Wi-Fi 7 routers for lower local wireless latency.

Weaknesses

  1. Very Limited Coverage Footprint: TIME is generally unavailable in landed housing estates, terraced homes, or rural townships due to their high-density property focus.
  2. Who should choose TIME: Condo, apartment, and high-rise residents who verify that TIME infrastructure is installed in their building.
  3. Who should avoid it: Anyone living in a landed house (terrace, semi-D, bungalow) where TIME cables do not run.

4. CelcomDigi Fibre

Following the merger of Celcom and Digi, CelcomDigi has positioned its fibre service as a value-driven ecosystem add-on for its massive mobile base.

Strengths

  1. Aggressive Postpaid Rebates: CelcomDigi postpaid users can unlock substantial monthly rebates on home fibre, dropping 300Mbps or 500Mbps plans down to highly competitive monthly rates.
  2. All-In-One Ecosystem: Options like the CelcomDigi One plans combine home fibre, unlimited 5G mobile lines, streaming subscriptions (e.g., Disney+ Hotstar), and device financing under a single bill.
  3. Wide Coverage Network: Operates across nationwide fibre networks (primarily TM HSBA), matching Unifi’s physical reach.

Weaknesses

  1. Complex Discount Structures: Standalone non-postpaid pricing is standard, so maximum value is locked behind holding a qualifying postpaid mobile plan.
  2. Who should choose CelcomDigi: Existing Celcom, Digi, or CelcomDigi mobile postpaid subscribers.
  3. Who should avoid it: Users who prefer to keep their mobile service and home broadband with completely separate providers.

Landed Houses

  1. Coverage Constraints: Infrastructure relies on physical overhead poles or underground ducting managed predominantly by TM.
  2. Port Limits: Fibre distribution point (DP) boxes on street poles have a fixed number of physical ports. If all ports on your pole are occupied, your installation will be delayed until TM expands capacity.

High-Rise Condominiums

  1. Infrastructure Management: High-rises use vertical fibre risers connected to an MDF (Main Distribution Frame) room in the basement.
  2. JMB/Management Rules: Building management (JMB/MC) determines which telcos are permitted to pull fiber cabling into the building's infrastructure.
  3. Port Availability: Always verify with your condo management or test your unit number online; even if the building has coverage, the specific floor box might be full.

Before You Apply: The 10-Point Checklist

Before signing a binding 24-month internet contract, review this safety checklist:

  1. [ ] 1. Verify Exact Unit Address Coverage: Do not rely on postcode checks. Confirm coverage down to your specific street name, house number, or condo block/unit number.
  2. [ ] 2. Check Physical Port Availability: Ask the installer or registration tool if an active port is free at your nearest Distribution Point (DP) box.
  3. [ ] 3. Confirm Contract Duration: Standard contracts are 24 months. Confirm the lock-in period and whether early termination penalties apply.
  4. [ ] 4. Check Post-Promotional Pricing: Ensure you know what the monthly fee will be after introductory discount periods (e.g., "RM99 for the first 6 months, RM139/month thereafter") expire.
  5. [ ] 5. Confirm Early Termination Charges: Most ISPs charge either a fixed penalty fee or the full remaining months of subscription if you cancel before the contract ends.
  6. [ ] 6. Inquire About Free Installation Limits: Standard installation (usually up to 20–30 meters of basic wiring) is free. Non-standard wiring (over-ceiling routing, trunking) incurs extra contractor charges.
  7. [ ] 7. Evaluate Router Specs: Check if the plan includes a basic router, a Wi-Fi 6 router, or a Mesh Wi-Fi system for wider home coverage.
  8. [ ] 8. Check Upload Speeds: If you stream, upload files, or host servers, verify that the upload speed meets your workflow requirements.
  9. [ ] 9. Check Relocation Rules: Ask if the provider allows you to transfer your contract to a new address if you move houses during the 24-month period.
  10. [ ] 10. Verify Mobile Postpaid Eligibility: If applying for Maxis or CelcomDigi bundles, ensure your mobile plan qualifies for the home internet rebate.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. What is the overall best fibre broadband in Malaysia?

There is no single best provider nationwide. TIME Dotcom offers the best value and highest speeds for high-rise condos, while Unifi is the most reliable option for landed houses and overall nationwide coverage.

2. Is Unifi better than Maxis Fibre?

Unifi and Maxis Fibre offer similar speeds and reliability because Maxis uses TM’s physical fibre network infrastructure (HSBA) for most of its coverage. Unifi is ideal for standalone internet or TV bundles, while Maxis Fibre is better if you bundle it with Maxis postpaid mobile lines for extra savings.

3. Is TIME Broadband better than Unifi?

Where TIME is available, it generally offers higher upload speeds, lower monthly rates per Mbps, and shorter/more flexible contract options. However, Unifi has a much wider coverage footprint across Malaysia.

4. Which fibre broadband is best for condos?

TIME Dotcom is typically the top choice for condo residents due to its symmetrical speed tiers and value pricing. If TIME is not supported in your building, Unifi or Maxis Fibre are strong secondary options.

5. Which fibre broadband is best for landed houses?

Unifi is the primary choice for landed houses due to Telekom Malaysia's extensive underground and pole-mounted optical cabling. Maxis Fibre and CelcomDigi Fibre are also available in landed homes connected via TM's wholesale network.

6. How many Mbps do I need for a typical family?

A 300Mbps plan is the recommended sweet spot for a typical Malaysian household of 3 to 5 people. It comfortably supports simultaneous 4K streaming, remote work video calls, and online gaming without congestion.

7. Is 300Mbps enough for 5 active users?

Yes. A 300Mbps connection provides ample bandwidth for 5 people streaming video, browsing social media, and attending online classes simultaneously. Upgrading to 500Mbps or 1Gbps is generally necessary only if multiple users download large files or stream live constantly.

8. Is fibre internet better than 5G Home Broadband?

Yes. Fixed fibre broadband uses physical glass cables, providing consistent speeds, weather resistance, and ultra-low latency. 5G Home Broadband relies on cellular wireless signals, which fluctuate depending on signal strength, weather, and local network traffic.

9. Can I switch broadband providers before my contract ends?

Yes, but early termination penalties usually apply. Most providers require you to pay a fixed cancellation fee or settle the remaining monthly fees of your 24-month contract term. Check your service agreement before initiating a switch.

10. How do I check fibre coverage at my home address?

You can check physical coverage by using address check tools provided by ISPs or by running a location query on platforms like NetBijak. Be sure to provide your exact house number, street name, or building unit number for accurate results.
