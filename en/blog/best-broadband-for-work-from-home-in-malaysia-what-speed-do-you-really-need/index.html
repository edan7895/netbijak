Best Broadband for Work From Home in Malaysia: What Speed Do You Really Need?

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

For most individuals working from home, a 100Mbps fibre connection provides sufficient bandwidth for standard professional tasks like video conferencing and cloud-based applications. While higher speeds like 300Mbps or 1Gbps are not typically necessary for a single user, they offer important capacity for households with multiple people streaming, gaming, or working simultaneously. It is crucial to remember that upload speed is just as important as download speed for tasks like file sharing and screen casting. If you experience poor connection quality, the issue may be your internal Wi-Fi signal, router placement, or interference rather than your broadband plan speed. Before upgrading your plan, consider testing a wired connection or optimizing your home network to ensure stability. Ultimately, choosing the right broadband depends on your specific household usage patterns and the infrastructure available in your area.

Working from home has become a normal part of life for many Malaysians.

Whether you spend your day on Zoom meetings, accessing cloud applications, uploading files, connecting through a VPN or collaborating with colleagues online, your broadband connection can have a direct impact on your workday.

But does working from home mean you need a 500Mbps or 1Gbps broadband plan?

Not necessarily.

For many people working from home alone, a reliable 100Mbps fibre connection can already provide enough bandwidth for video meetings, cloud applications and everyday work.

The more important question is not simply:

“How fast is the broadband plan?”

It is:

“How many people are using the connection, and what are they doing at the same time?”

What Broadband Speed Do You Need for Working From Home?

For a single person working from home, 100Mbps is generally more than enough for common WFH activities.

Zoom's current requirements illustrate why. For a 1-to-1 720p HD video call, Zoom recommends around 1.2Mbps upload and download, while a 1080p group video call requires around 3.8Mbps upload and 3.0Mbps download.

This means a 100Mbps connection has considerably more bandwidth than a single normal video meeting requires.

However, your broadband connection is shared by the entire household.

If someone else is watching 4K video, downloading a large game or uploading files while you are in a video meeting, the available bandwidth can become more important.

100Mbps vs 300Mbps for WFH

For many home workers, the difference between 100Mbps and 300Mbps is less about whether you can work from home and more about how much capacity your household has.

A 300Mbps plan does not automatically make Zoom meetings three times better.

Instead, the extra bandwidth becomes useful when multiple activities happen at the same time.

Why Upload Speed Matters for WFH

One thing consumers often overlook when comparing broadband plans is upload speed.

Download speed is important when receiving information from the internet.

Upload speed becomes particularly relevant when you are:

  1. Uploading large files
  2. Sending video
  3. Using cloud storage
  4. Backing up files
  5. Sharing your screen
  6. Uploading content
  7. Working with remote systems

For example, Zoom's current requirements for 1080p video calls specify around 3.8Mbps upload and 3.0Mbps download for both 1-to-1 and group video calls.

That does not mean you need a 3.8Mbps broadband plan.

It means your connection needs to have sufficient available upload capacity during the call.

This is one reason consumers should not compare broadband plans based only on the advertised download speed.

Is 100Mbps Enough for One Person Working From Home?

For many users, yes.

If you are the only person working from home and mainly use:

  1. Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace
  2. Zoom / Google Meet / Teams
  3. Email
  4. Web applications
  5. VPN
  6. Cloud storage
  7. Web browsing

100Mbps can already provide plenty of bandwidth.

The bigger concern may be connection stability and Wi-Fi performance rather than raw speed.

Zoom itself recommends checking your Wi-Fi connection and, where possible, trying a wired connection when experiencing latency, frozen screens or meeting disconnections.

When Is 300Mbps Better for WFH?

300Mbps becomes more attractive when your home has multiple active users.

For example:

You — Zoom meeting
Your partner — Microsoft Teams
Child — Online class
Another family member — Netflix
PC — Downloading a large file

A 300Mbps connection provides more overall bandwidth for these simultaneous activities.

It does not necessarily make each individual activity three times faster.

Instead, it gives the household more room to use the connection at the same time.

What About 500Mbps or 1Gbps?

For most people working from home, 500Mbps or 1Gbps is not necessary simply because they work remotely.

Higher speeds become more relevant when your household has:

  1. Many simultaneous users
  2. Multiple 4K streams
  3. Large file transfers
  4. Frequent large downloads
  5. Content creation
  6. Cloud backups
  7. Multiple WFH users
  8. Heavy gaming
  9. Many connected devices

For example, Maxis currently positions its 500Mbps plan for moderate-to-heavy users, including gaming, study and work-from-home setups involving around 6–8 devices.

That is a useful illustration of how higher-speed plans are generally more about household capacity than simply making one person's Zoom call faster.

Fibre vs 5G Home Broadband for WFH

If working from home is important to you, fixed fibre broadband is generally worth considering where available.

The key advantage is that you have a fixed connection into the home rather than relying on mobile network conditions.

However, 5G Home Broadband can still be useful in situations such as:

  1. Fibre is unavailable
  2. You are renting
  3. You need a flexible installation
  4. You move frequently
  5. Your location has strong 5G coverage

The important point is to look at the actual performance available at your address rather than assuming one technology will always be better.

Wi-Fi Can Be the Real Problem

Sometimes users upgrade from 100Mbps to 500Mbps but continue experiencing:

“Slow internet.”

The problem may not be the broadband plan.

It could be:

  1. Weak Wi-Fi signal
  2. Router location
  3. Thick walls
  4. Interference
  5. Old Wi-Fi equipment
  6. Too many connected devices
  7. Poor placement of a mesh system

Zoom recommends moving closer to the Wi-Fi router or using a wired connection when dealing with home Wi-Fi problems.

So before upgrading your broadband speed, check whether the problem is actually your home network.

Which Broadband Providers Should WFH Users Compare?

There is no single provider that is automatically the best for every WFH user.

When comparing broadband plans in Malaysia, consider providers such as:

  1. Unifi
  2. Maxis
  3. CelcomDigi
  4. TIME
  5. Other fibre providers available at your address

For example, CelcomDigi currently offers 100Mbps, 300Mbps, 500Mbps, 1Gbps and 2Gbps fibre plans, with different upload speeds and equipment depending on the plan.

Maxis currently offers plans ranging from 100Mbps to 2Gbps, with its current plan positioning varying according to household size and usage.

The important point is that availability varies by location.

A plan that looks perfect on paper is not useful if the provider cannot install it at your home.

What Should You Look for in a WFH Broadband Plan?

Instead of focusing only on Mbps, compare:

1. Download Speed

Important for browsing, downloading files, streaming and general internet usage.

2. Upload Speed

Particularly important for video calls, cloud backups, large uploads and content-related work.

3. Connection Stability

A stable 100Mbps connection can be more useful for work than an unstable faster connection.

4. Latency

Important for video calls, remote desktop, VPN and other real-time applications.

5. Wi-Fi Router

A good broadband plan can still perform poorly if the router cannot provide adequate coverage.

6. Contract Period

Check whether you are committing to 12, 24 or another contract period.

7. Total Cost

Look beyond promotional pricing and check the normal monthly fee after the promotion ends.

8. Fibre Availability

Always check whether the provider is actually available at your address.

A simple guide:

A household can work perfectly well on a lower-speed plan if its actual usage is light.

NetBijak's Take

There is no single “best broadband” for working from home.

For one person working from home, 100Mbps fibre may already provide more than enough bandwidth for video meetings, cloud applications, VPN and everyday work.

For households with several people working or studying at the same time, 300Mbps or higher can provide more breathing room.

But before paying for a faster plan, we recommend looking beyond the advertised download speed.

Upload speed, connection stability, latency, Wi-Fi coverage and actual availability at your address can all affect your working experience.

In our view, the best WFH broadband plan is not necessarily the fastest one.

It is the plan that provides enough capacity and stability for your household without making you pay for speed you do not actually use.
