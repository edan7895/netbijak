Student Broadband Guide: Cheap Internet Options Near Malaysian Universities

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

Choosing the right broadband for a shared student household involves balancing cost, contract length, and connection speed rather than just picking the cheapest plan. As a general guide, households should aim for 100-200Mbps for two people, scaling up to 500Mbps for larger groups of up to eight. TIME Fibre is often the most student-friendly choice due to its shorter 12-month contract and high upload speeds, provided it is available at your specific address. While Unifi and Maxis offer competitive speeds, students must carefully weigh their longer contract commitments against their actual rental duration. Regardless of the provider, managing heavy traffic through router Quality of Service features is essential to prevent gaming latency and connectivity issues. Always verify service coverage at your specific residence before committing to any long-term broadband plan.

If you are renting a house or apartment with other students, the cheapest broadband plan is not always the best choice. For a 4–8 person household, monthly cost per person, contract length, Fibre availability, upload speed and router performance can matter more than simply buying the lowest-priced plan.

How Much Internet Speed Does a Student House Need?

A useful starting point is:

  1. 1–2 students: 100–200Mbps
  2. 3–4 students: 200–300Mbps
  3. 5–8 students: 300–500Mbps

These are practical guidelines, not hard limits. A four-person house where everyone streams 4K video and joins video calls at the same time can put more pressure on the connection than six light users.

For shared accommodation, the bigger issue is usually simultaneous usage. One person downloading a 100GB game can affect everyone else if the router does not manage the traffic properly.

TIME Fibre: The Most Student-Friendly Option If Available

TIME is particularly interesting for students because its dedicated Student Plans are designed around a 12-month commitment.

The 200Mbps plan costs RM119/month, while the 600Mbps plan costs RM159/month. The latter provides 600Mbps download and 500Mbps upload. (Source: TIME — Time Home Internet for Students)

For a shared student house, that upload speed can be useful when several people are:

  1. Uploading assignments
  2. Using Google Drive or OneDrive
  3. Attending video meetings
  4. Streaming
  5. Uploading large files
  6. Gaming while other users are active

The 12-month commitment can also make more sense for students renting for one academic year than signing a substantially longer contract.

The catch is simple: TIME coverage is not available at every address.

Unifi: A Practical Option for Shared Student Houses

Unifi's 300Mbps and 500Mbps plans are suitable for many shared households.

The current 300Mbps plan is RM129/month with 300Mbps download and 50Mbps upload, while the 500Mbps plan is RM149/month with 500Mbps download and 100Mbps upload. Unifi itself positions these plans for households with multiple connected devices. (Source: Unifi — Unifi Home Shield)

For students, the main issue is the contract commitment.

Current Unifi plan pages show 27-month contracts for these plans. That is significantly longer than a typical one-year student rental, so you should check the exact contract before applying. (Source: Unifi — 300Mbps / 500Mbps Home Fibre Plans)

If your tenancy is only 12 months, a long contract can become an important cost consideration.

Maxis Fibre: Worth Checking If You Already Use Maxis

Maxis Fibre lists 300Mbps at RM139/month and 500Mbps at RM159/month under its current standard Home Fibre pricing. New installations also include a WiFi 6 router under the current offering. (Source: Maxis — Maxis Home Fibre FAQ)

Maxis can become more attractive when you are already using Maxis Postpaid because eligible customers may receive bundled pricing.

For example, current Maxis bundle offers include discounted Fibre pricing for eligible Maxis Postpaid customers. (Source: Maxis — Maxis Postpaid + Fibre Bundle)

However, do not assume the bundle automatically makes Maxis cheaper. Calculate the total monthly bill, including the mobile plan you need to maintain eligibility.

The Real Problem in an 8-Person Student House

Imagine eight students sharing a 500Mbps Fibre connection.

At 8pm:

  1. Student A is playing an online game.
  2. Student B is on a Google Meet call.
  3. Student C is watching Netflix.
  4. Student D is watching YouTube.
  5. Student E is downloading a Steam game.
  6. Student F is uploading a large assignment folder.
  7. Students G and H are browsing and using social media.

The Fibre connection may have enough total bandwidth.

But the gaming PC can still experience high latency or packet loss because large downloads and uploads are filling the router's queues.

This is where QoS becomes useful.

What Is QoS?

QoS (Quality of Service) is a router feature that manages traffic when the connection becomes congested.

It does not increase your broadband speed.

Instead, it helps decide which traffic should be served first when multiple devices are competing for the same connection.

For gaming, this matters because stable latency is usually more important than having an enormous download speed.

A 300Mbps connection with good traffic management can provide a better gaming experience than a much faster connection that is constantly saturated by downloads.

Do not make every device HIGH PRIORITY. If everything is prioritised, the router has little meaningful priority management left to perform.

For a 300Mbps connection, you can also configure the QoS bandwidth slightly below the real maximum—for example, around 270–285Mbps download and 45–47Mbps upload on a 300/50Mbps connection. This is a practical starting point, not a universal setting; the ideal value depends on the router and actual line performance.

Wi-Fi Can Still Be the Bottleneck

Even with good Fibre, Wi-Fi can become the weak link.

This is especially common in:

  1. Large apartments
  2. Double-storey student houses
  3. Houses with thick concrete walls
  4. Rooms far from the router
  5. Houses with 8 or more active users

For gaming, use Ethernet whenever practical.

If Ethernet is not possible, consider placing the router in a central location or using a properly configured mesh Wi-Fi system.

Do not assume upgrading from 300Mbps to 500Mbps will automatically fix bad Wi-Fi coverage.

Should Students Buy 5G Home WiFi Instead?

5G Home WiFi can make sense for students who:

  1. Rent for only a few months
  2. Cannot get Fibre at the property
  3. Need an Internet connection quickly
  4. Move frequently

The major advantage is flexibility.

The downside is consistency.

5G performance can change depending on:

  1. Indoor signal strength
  2. Distance from the cell site
  3. Network congestion
  4. Building construction
  5. Time of day

For competitive gaming or a house with heavy simultaneous usage, Fibre is generally the safer option when it is available.

Another consideration is CGNAT/private IP addressing, which can affect some gaming, remote-access, hosting and port-forwarding use cases.

Is a 12-Month Broadband Contract Better for Students?

If your tenancy is only one academic year, a 12-month Fibre plan can be much easier to manage.

Consider this before signing:

Rental period: 12 months

Broadband contract: 24–27 months

That creates a problem when you move out.

You may have to:

  1. Transfer the service
  2. Pay early termination charges
  3. Find someone to take over the service, if permitted
  4. Continue paying after leaving the property

This is why TIME's current 12-month Student Plans can be particularly relevant to university students. (Source: TIME — Time Home Internet for Students)

How Much Does Broadband Cost Per Student?

The monthly bill becomes much easier to justify when several people share it.

For example:

RM159 plan shared by 8 students

RM159 ÷ 8 = RM19.88 per person/month

RM129 plan shared by 4 students

RM129 ÷ 4 = RM32.25 per person/month

RM159 plan shared by 6 students

RM159 ÷ 6 = RM26.50 per person/month

But do not choose purely based on the lowest cost per person.

A cheap plan with poor Wi-Fi coverage, insufficient upload capacity or a contract that extends beyond your tenancy can cost more in the long run.

What Should You Check Before Renting?

Before signing the rental agreement, ask the landlord or previous tenant:

  1. Is Fibre available at this exact address?
  2. Is there already a Fibre termination point in the house?
  3. Where is the current router located?
  4. Is there already an active broadband account?
  5. How long is the broadband contract?
  6. Can the router be placed in a central location?
  7. Are Ethernet ports available in the bedrooms?

The exact address matters because Fibre availability is not determined simply by whether the neighbourhood has Fibre coverage.

Student Broadband Checklist

Before choosing a plan, check these six things:

1. Fibre Coverage

Check the exact rental address, not just the university area.

2. Contract Length

Match the broadband contract to your expected rental period.

3. Upload Speed

Important for video calls, cloud storage, file uploads and some gaming/remote-access situations.

4. Cost Per Person

Divide the total monthly bill by the number of people actually sharing it.

5. Router & Wi-Fi

A fast Fibre plan cannot compensate for poor Wi-Fi coverage.

6. QoS

For a busy shared house, traffic management can matter more than simply upgrading to a faster package.


NetBijak's Take

For students sharing a house, do not choose broadband by download speed alone.

The better decision process is:

Check Fibre Coverage → Check Contract Length → Compare Upload Speed → Calculate Cost Per Person → Check Router/Wi-Fi → Configure QoS

If you are renting for one academic year, a 12-month student Fibre plan can be particularly attractive because it reduces the mismatch between your rental period and broadband contract.

If TIME is available, its current Student Plans are worth checking first. If it is unavailable, Unifi and Maxis remain practical alternatives depending on the exact address, contract and any eligible bundle discounts.

For an 8-person house, spending a little more on the right plan and configuring QoS can be more useful than simply buying the highest advertised speed.
