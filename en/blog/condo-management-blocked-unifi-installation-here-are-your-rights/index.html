Condo Management Blocked Unifi Installation? Here Are Your Rights

Can Condo Management Stop Your Unifi Installation?

Imagine this.

You have checked your address. Unifi Fibre is available.

You sign up.

The Unifi installer arrives at your condominium — only to be told:

“Management does not allow the installation.”

You may immediately wonder:

“If I’m paying for the internet and Unifi is available at my address, can the condo management really stop me?”

The answer is not as simple as yes or no.

For high-rise properties, Unifi’s current installation guidance says residents need to obtain consent from the building management office because fibre installation may involve drilling. Unifi’s terms also specifically address situations where installation cannot proceed because of building management issues, internal wiring or denied access to the building. (Unifi)

But this does not mean management automatically has unlimited power to reject every fibre installation.

The important question is why the installation is being blocked.

1. Why Does Unifi Need Condo Management Approval?

A fibre installation in a condominium may involve more than your own unit.

The installer may need access to:

  1. Common areas
  2. Telecommunications rooms
  3. Cable risers
  4. Existing ducts
  5. Building entrances
  6. Corridors
  7. Other shared infrastructure

These areas may be considered common property rather than part of your individual parcel.

Under Malaysia’s strata management framework, common property is managed by the relevant management body, such as the Joint Management Body (JMB) or Management Corporation (MC). The Malaysian Bar explains that the management body is responsible for the management and administration of common property. (Malaysian Bar)

That is why you cannot always tell an installer:

“It’s my condo, just drill whatever you need.”

If the work involves common property, building management may have legitimate authority over access and installation arrangements.

2. Does This Mean Management Can Reject Unifi for Any Reason?

Not necessarily.

This is where residents need to be careful.

There is a difference between:

“Management approval is required before installation.”

and:

“Management can permanently prevent residents from getting a telecommunications service for any reason.”

Those are not the same thing.

The applicable strata by-laws bind the developer, JMB, MC, parcel owners and occupiers, and the management body has responsibilities relating to common property. (MyLaw)

So management can legitimately impose requirements relating to:

  1. Safety
  2. Access
  3. Drilling
  4. Cable routes
  5. Protection of common property
  6. Working hours
  7. Contractor registration
  8. Security procedures
  9. Telecommunications-room access

But if management simply says:

“No Unifi. We don’t allow it.”

you should ask why.

Ask for the reason in writing and ask which house rule, by-law, building policy or technical restriction the decision is based on.

3. What If Management Says the Building Already Has Another Fibre Provider?

This is another situation residents may encounter.

For example, your condo already has another broadband provider, and management tells you:

“We already have fibre. You cannot install Unifi.”

That does not automatically answer the question.

Different telecommunications providers may use different network infrastructure and installation arrangements.

The key issue is whether allowing Unifi would create a legitimate problem involving:

  1. Common property
  2. Existing cable infrastructure
  3. Safety
  4. Access
  5. Building damage
  6. Technical feasibility
  7. Building rules

A blanket “we already have another provider” explanation is different from a documented technical or building-management reason.

4. What Does Unifi’s Own Installation Policy Say?

This is particularly important because Unifi’s own documents acknowledge the role of building management.

Unifi’s installation guideline states that residents in high-rise buildings such as condominiums or apartments are required to obtain consent from the building management office where installation may involve drilling. (Unifi)

Unifi’s current terms go further.

They state that if TM or its authorised contractor cannot proceed with an installation at a high-rise building because of building-management issues — including internal wiring problems or being denied access — the customer is responsible for resolving the issue with the building owner or management corporation within 21 days. If the issue is not resolved, TM may cancel the installation or provisioning. (Unifi)

So if your installer tells you:

“Management rejected the installation.”

don’t simply blame the installer.

You need to find out exactly what management rejected and why.

5. Ask Management for the Reason in Writing

This is probably the most useful step.

Instead of arguing at the management counter, politely ask:

“Could you please provide the reason why the Unifi installation is not allowed, and the relevant house rule, by-law or building policy?”

You should also ask:

  1. Is drilling the issue?
  2. Is access to a telecom room required?
  3. Is the cable route not approved?
  4. Is there an existing infrastructure restriction?
  5. Does management require a specific contractor?
  6. Does management require an application form?
  7. Is there a fee or deposit?
  8. Is the installation prohibited permanently or only until certain requirements are met?

A verbal:

“Cannot.”

does not tell you enough.

A written explanation gives you something concrete to bring back to Unifi or, if necessary, the relevant strata authority.

6. Can You Ask Unifi to Deal Directly With Management?

Yes, this is worth trying.

If you have already subscribed and the installation is blocked, contact Unifi and explain that the issue is specifically related to building management.

Ask Unifi:

  1. What installation method is required?
  2. Where does the fibre need to enter the building?
  3. Does the installer need access to common property?
  4. Does Unifi have an approved method for your building?
  5. Can Unifi communicate with the building management?
  6. Can Unifi provide technical documentation for the management office?

Unifi provides customer support through its channels, including Live Chat and the TM100 hotline. (Unifi)

This can be much more productive than arguing with the management office yourself.

7. What If Management Is Refusing to Give Any Reason?

This is where you should move from an argument to a formal process.

Keep records of:

  1. Your Unifi order number
  2. Installation appointment
  3. Installer’s message
  4. Management’s response
  5. Emails
  6. WhatsApp messages
  7. Photos, if relevant
  8. Any written building rules
  9. Any additional fees requested

Then ask Unifi to record that the installation is being blocked by building management.

For communications-service complaints, Malaysia has an established consumer complaint framework. The Consumer Forum Malaysia (CFM) operates under the communications and multimedia consumer framework, while MCMC provides complaint channels for communications-related matters. ((CFM) Consumer Forum Malaysia)

If the dispute is actually about strata management/common property, rather than the quality of Unifi’s service itself, you may also need to consider the relevant strata-management authority or legal advice rather than assuming MCMC will decide the building-management dispute.

8. What Are Your “Rights” as a Condo Resident?

This is the part where wording matters.

You should not assume:

“I have the right to install any cable anywhere I want.”

You don’t.

Common property is subject to strata management rules.

But you also should not assume:

“Management can reject any internet installation whenever it wants.”

That is also too broad.

Your practical rights include the ability to:

Ask for a reason

You can ask why the installation is being rejected.

Ask for the applicable rule

Ask whether the restriction comes from a by-law, house rule, building policy or technical requirement.

Ask for a workable alternative

For example, management may prohibit one cable route but allow another approved route.

Ask Unifi to clarify the technical requirements

This helps determine whether the proposed installation is actually compliant with building requirements.

Escalate appropriately

If the issue concerns a strata-management dispute, consider the relevant strata-management channels or professional legal advice.

The exact legal position can depend on your building, its rules, the type of work required and the state/federal territory involved. The Strata Management Act 2013 framework does not mean every condominium dispute has the same answer. (MSIC Malaysia)

9. What If You Are Renting the Condo?

If you are a tenant, there may be another layer.

You should check your tenancy arrangement and whether the landlord needs to approve certain alterations.

You may also need:

  1. Landlord consent
  2. Building management approval
  3. Unifi installation approval
  4. Access arrangements

Don’t assume that being the Unifi account holder gives you unrestricted authority to modify the property.

10. What Should You Do If Your Unifi Installation Is Blocked?

Use this simple process:

Step 1 — Ask why

Get the exact reason from management.

Step 2 — Ask for the rule

Request the relevant house rule, by-law or building policy.

Step 3 — Contact Unifi

Tell Unifi the exact reason given by management.

Step 4 — Ask for an alternative installation method

Sometimes the problem is the proposed cable route rather than Unifi itself.

Step 5 — Get everything in writing

Keep the emails, WhatsApp messages and installation records.

Step 6 — Escalate if necessary

If the dispute concerns telecommunications service, consider the appropriate MCMC/CFM complaint channel. If it concerns the management of common property or strata governance, consider the appropriate strata-management route.

Don’t Fight With Your Condo Management First

The easiest way to make this situation worse is to start with:

“You have no right to stop me.”

Instead, start with:

“Could you please explain the reason and let me know what requirements I need to meet for the installation to be approved?”

That changes the conversation from a confrontation into a problem-solving discussion.

In many cases, management may not actually be against Unifi.

They may simply want:

  1. A specific cable route
  2. No drilling in certain areas
  3. A contractor registration
  4. Access arrangements
  5. Protection for common property
  6. Work to be carried out at specific times


NetBijak Tips

Don’t assume “management approval required” means “management can say no forever.”

At the same time, don’t assume you can force an installer to work on common property without management approval.

The smart approach is:

Check the rule → understand the reason → ask Unifi for the technical solution → get everything in writing → escalate through the correct channel if necessary.

And before signing up for a broadband plan, remember:

Availability on the provider’s website does not always mean installation can be completed without building-level approval.

For condo residents, the best broadband plan is one that is available at your address and practically installable in your building.
