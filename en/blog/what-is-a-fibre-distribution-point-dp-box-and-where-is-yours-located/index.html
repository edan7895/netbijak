What Is a Fibre Distribution Point (DP) Box and Where Is Yours Located?

✨ NetBijak AI Summary

A Fibre Distribution Point (DP) is the essential link in the network chain that connects high-speed fibre optic cables to your home. These units take various forms, including pole-mounted aerial boxes, ground-level pedestals, and concealed underground or internal building infrastructure. Because the specific DP serving your property may not be the one closest to your door, technicians must verify the network route to confirm serviceability. If you are asked to locate your DP, the most effective method is to take a clear, well-lit photo of the entire unit along with its identifying label. Avoid attempting to decode network numbering systems yourself, as these labels are specific to the provider’s internal mapping. Ultimately, the presence of a nearby box does not guarantee an immediate connection, as site-specific assessments are always required for a successful installation.

If you are waiting for a Fibre installation or trying to explain your connection to a technician, knowing where the Fibre Distribution Point (DP) is can make the process much easier. In Malaysia, a DP can be installed in different locations depending on the network design, including aerial, underground, pedestal and wall-mounted arrangements. (Source: TM — Self Learning Notes, Section 1.7 Fibre Distribution Point.) TM official Fibre technical notes

What Is a Fibre Distribution Point?

A Fibre Distribution Point (FDP) is the distribution point used to connect drop fibre towards a customer's premises. TM's technical material places the FDP between the Fibre Distribution Cabinet (FDC) and the customer side of the FTTH network.

TM also documents the internal components of an aerial FDP, including an adaptor panel, cable inlet, cable outlet, protection sleeve holder, optical splitter and fibre organiser.

A simplified residential Fibre path can be understood as:

FDC → FDP → Drop Fibre → FTB → FWS → ONU

TM's technical notes specifically describe the drop fibre as running from the FDP to the Fiber Termination Box (FTB) at the customer's premises.

1. Pole-Mounted or Aerial DP

The DP that most homeowners notice first is the aerial or pole-mounted DP.

TM's technical material includes an Aerial Fibre Distribution Point and shows it as an enclosure used to distribute fibre connections.

For landed homes, Unifi's current installation guidance also shows a specific scenario where the DP is on the pole. It states that direct cabling from the pole to the entry wall is one of its standard installation scenarios for landed properties.

From the road, you may see a small enclosure mounted on a pole with Fibre cables entering and leaving the equipment.

You do not need to open the enclosure to identify it. The useful information is the location, visible label and nearby reference points.

2. Ground-Level or Pedestal DP

Not every DP is mounted on a pole.

TM's technical notes explicitly state that, besides an aerial DP, there are also underground DP, pedestal DP and wall-mounted DP types.

Unifi's installation guidance also has a specific scenario showing the Distribution Point on the ground. Its installation method then depends on how the cable needs to be routed from the DP to the customer's property.

For a homeowner, this means the relevant Fibre DP may appear as ground-level equipment rather than a box high on a pole.

Do not identify a cabinet simply by its colour or appearance. The better approach is to check any visible network label and let the technician confirm whether it is the serving DP.

3. Underground DP: You May Not See an Above-Ground Box

TM's technical material also recognises underground DP as one of the possible FDP arrangements.

In an underground deployment, the relevant Fibre infrastructure may not be visible as a cabinet beside the road. Part of the network can be routed underground, with access provided through underground infrastructure.

TM also documents FTTH installation using overhead and underground cable routes, showing that the path between network infrastructure and a customer's premises can differ depending on the site.

So, not seeing an obvious Fibre box outside your house does not automatically mean there is no Fibre distribution infrastructure serving the property.

4. What About Condos and Apartments?

High-rise Fibre installations can be different from landed properties.

TM's FTTH training material includes dedicated sections for high-rise buildings, telecommunications rooms, riser rooms and internal building cabling.

That means the relevant Fibre distribution infrastructure for a condominium or apartment may not be a roadside box.

Depending on the building design, technicians may need to work with infrastructure inside the building rather than simply connecting from a street-side DP.

This is one reason a high-rise Fibre installation can require different site arrangements from a landed house.

5. How Do You Read the Number on a DP?

You may see a combination of letters and numbers on a Fibre enclosure.

The important point is: do not assume that every part of the number has a universal meaning.

There is no reason to treat a label such as:

ABC / 0123

as though the first part must represent a street, the second part must represent a Port, or the whole number must directly correspond to your house.

The safest method is to record the entire label exactly as shown.

The Better Way: Take a Photo

If a technician asks for the DP number, send:

Close-up photo

Shows the full label or identification.

Wider photo

Shows the complete DP and its pole, pedestal or wall location.

Location reference

Include the nearest house number, junction, street sign or another permanent landmark.

This is more useful than trying to interpret a network code yourself.

6. How to Find the DP Near a Landed House

For a terrace house, semi-D or bungalow, start by checking the Fibre infrastructure around the road outside your property.

Look for:

  1. Pole-mounted Fibre enclosures
  2. Ground-level or pedestal Fibre equipment
  3. Fibre cables running along the road
  4. Network labels attached to Fibre equipment
  5. Telecommunications poles and related infrastructure

But do not assume the closest box is automatically the box serving your house.

The actual Fibre route is what matters. A DP further down the road may serve your property, while a closer-looking box may belong to another network section.

If you are unsure, photograph the candidate DP and give the location to the technician.

7. Does a DP Near Your House Mean You Can Get Fibre?

No.

A DP close to your property shows that Fibre distribution infrastructure is present nearby, but it does not by itself confirm that a Fibre installation can proceed.

Unifi states that its technicians assess the home or building layout, the location of the Distribution Point and the cabling requirements before determining the appropriate installation method.

For landed properties, Unifi currently lists different installation scenarios depending on whether the DP is on the ground or on a pole and how the cable needs to reach the property.

So:

DP nearby ≠ guaranteed installation

and

Fibre coverage ≠ automatically the same thing as an installable connection.

8. Why Does the DP Location Matter?

The DP is part of the physical route used to bring Fibre towards the premises.

Unifi's installation guidance states that technicians consider the DP location and cabling requirements when deciding the suitable installation method.

For example, its current landed-property guidance distinguishes between:

DP on a pole → direct cabling to the entry wall

and

DP on a pole → underground/surface cabling route

with different installation classifications.

This is why a technician may ask you to identify the DP before installation work starts.

9. The Easiest Way to Tell a Technician Where the DP Is

Avoid saying:

“The DP is somewhere near my house.”

Give a fixed reference point.

For example:

“The DP is on the pole opposite House No. 18, about 20 metres from my gate. The label says ABC/0123.”

Then attach a photo.

The ideal information is:

DP label: exact text shown on the box.

Location: pole, ground-level cabinet, wall or other visible position.

Reference point: house number, junction, street sign or permanent landmark.

Photos: close-up plus a wider location shot.

This gives the technician something they can verify on site instead of relying on a vague description.

10. Do Not Open the DP Yourself

A DP is Fibre network infrastructure, not a customer-access box.

TM's technical documentation shows that an FDP contains Fibre distribution components such as connector panels, cable routes, optical splitters and Fibre organisation components.

You do not need to open it to help with an installation.

Do not:

  1. Open the enclosure
  2. Disconnect Fibre connectors
  3. Pull or bend Fibre cables
  4. Move the equipment
  5. Try to identify individual customer connections

Your useful role is simply to identify the equipment and provide its location.

Leave the connection, testing and Port identification to the network or ISP technician.

NetBijak’s Take

You do not need to understand the entire Fibre network to help with an installation.

For most homeowners, three things are enough:

Find the likely DP. Photograph the label. Record a permanent landmark.

That gives the technician useful information without the need to touch the Fibre infrastructure itself.
