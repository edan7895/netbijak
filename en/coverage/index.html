Why Fibre Availability Depends on Your Exact Address

Why does fibre availability depend on your exact address?

Because fibre serviceability can differ by building infrastructure, internal wiring, available network capacity and installation conditions. A nearby house or unit may be serviceable while your exact address is not.

When checking broadband availability in Malaysia, simply entering a postcode or checking an area on a coverage map is not always enough. Major providers use address-level information to determine whether a specific property can be served.

For example, Unifi asks customers to enter a full address and states that fibre availability can be affected by infrastructure limitations, in-building wiring and other factors. Its coverage checker also notes that area coverage does not guarantee service availability at a specific address. (Unifi Coverage Checker)

Maxis similarly requires information such as unit/block, postcode, state and building or street name when checking Home Fibre availability. (Maxis Coverage Checker)

TIME also checks availability based on the building or street and allows users to add their address to a waiting list when the service is not yet available. (TIME Coverage Checker)

Fibre Coverage ≠ Installation Availability

This is an important distinction.

Fibre coverage means the provider's network reaches your area or building.

Fibre availability means your specific property can actually be connected.

Even when fibre coverage exists, installation may still depend on available ports, existing fibre infrastructure, internal building wiring and other technical conditions. Unifi explicitly advises users to apply online to check port availability after coverage is confirmed.

Why can your neighbour get fibre but you cannot?

This is particularly common in apartments, condominiums and multi-unit buildings.

Two units in the same building may not have identical network connections or available capacity. A provider may have fibre infrastructure in the building, but the specific unit may still require further verification.

That is why broadband providers normally ask for detailed address information instead of relying only on postcode or neighbourhood coverage.

What happens when there is no available fibre port?

A lack of available capacity does not necessarily mean fibre will never become available.

You can ask the provider whether your address can be placed on a waiting or demand list for additional local network capacity, such as DP box expansion.

Another option is to check whether 5G Home Wireless is available at your location. Unlike fixed fibre, 5G Home Wireless can provide a plug-and-play broadband connection without requiring a fibre installation. Maxis, for example, currently provides separate Home Fibre and 5G Home WiFi availability checks.

What should you check before applying?

Before choosing a broadband plan, check these three things:

1. Area coverage

Is the ISP's network available in your area?

2. Exact-address availability

Can the provider actually serve your particular unit, house or building?

3. Port / installation availability

Is there currently enough local network capacity and infrastructure to complete the installation?

This is why a broadband comparison should not stop at advertised price and speed. A RM89 or RM99 fibre plan is irrelevant if the service cannot actually be installed at your address.

NetBijak’s Take

Coverage is only the first step. Your exact address determines whether you can actually get the service.

Before comparing broadband prices or speed, check serviceability at your actual unit or house. If fibre is unavailable because of local capacity, ask about expansion or waiting-list options, then consider 5G Home Wireless as an alternative.
