Quick Verdict

Unifi Home 500Mbps is aimed at households where high bandwidth is being consumed by several devices at the same time, rather than users who simply want faster downloads on one laptop. With 500Mbps download and 100Mbps upload, it has considerably more capacity for simultaneous streaming, gaming, video calls and large file transfers.

The price is higher than the 300Mbps tier, so the question is whether your household can actually use the additional capacity. For a home with only a few active users, 500Mbps can easily become unused bandwidth.

Performance & MCMC 90% Benchmark

The official specification for this tier is:

500Mbps download / 100Mbps upload.

Using the 90% Fibre throughput benchmark:

500Mbps × 90% = 450Mbps

So 450Mbps download is the useful reference point when evaluating a properly tested 500Mbps fibre connection.

For upload:

100Mbps × 90% = 90Mbps

This gives a useful reference of approximately 90Mbps upload under the same 90% calculation.

However, these figures should not be treated as a guarantee that every Wi-Fi test must produce 450/90Mbps.

For troubleshooting, use a wired Gigabit Ethernet connection and repeat the tests under controlled conditions.

A 500Mbps plan makes poor testing methodology even more obvious. A phone connected to a distant access point can show significantly less than 500Mbps even while the fibre service itself is performing normally.

Hardware & Technical Traps

500Mbps changes the minimum hardware standard

At this tier, 100Mbps Ethernet is no longer merely inconvenient—it is a hard bottleneck.

If your device, adapter, switch or router port negotiates at only 100Mbps:

500Mbps fibre → 100Mbps Ethernet → maximum practical throughput ≈ 100Mbps

That makes a Gigabit Ethernet connection the sensible minimum for wired devices that need to take advantage of the plan.

Cat5e is still sufficient

You do not need Cat6 specifically for 500Mbps.

A properly installed Cat5e cable supports Gigabit Ethernet, which provides enough capacity for a 500Mbps internet connection.

The important issue is not whether the cable says Cat5e or Cat6. It is whether the complete Ethernet link successfully negotiates at 1Gbps.

If Windows or another network diagnostic tool reports a 100Mbps link speed, investigate the cable, connector, Ethernet adapter, switch or router port before blaming the broadband connection.

Wi-Fi 6 can handle the tier—but the client matters

Unifi includes a Wi-Fi 6 Combo Box with the 500Mbps plan.

That provides suitable wireless hardware, but your client device still determines how much of the 500Mbps connection it can actually use.

A newer Wi-Fi 6 laptop close to the router can make much better use of the available bandwidth than an older 2.4GHz-only device.

This is why “Wi-Fi 6 included” should not be interpreted as “500Mbps everywhere in the house.”

The 100Mbps Upload Makes This Tier More Interesting

The biggest technical difference between this package and lower tiers is not just the jump from 300Mbps to 500Mbps download.

The official specification provides 100Mbps upload.

That makes the plan more useful for households that generate as well as consume large amounts of data.

Examples include:

  1. Cloud backup
  2. Large video uploads
  3. Sending large work files
  4. Remote NAS access
  5. Livestreaming
  6. Frequent media production
  7. Multiple users uploading simultaneously

A 500/100Mbps connection still has asymmetric bandwidth, but the 100Mbps upload ceiling is substantially more useful for data-heavy households than a lower upload allocation.

For ordinary Netflix, YouTube, browsing and gaming, however, the additional upload capacity may not produce a noticeable difference.

Where 500Mbps Actually Helps

The strongest argument for 500Mbps is concurrency.

Imagine a household where:

  1. One person is downloading a large game
  2. Two people are watching high-resolution video
  3. Another person is on a video conference
  4. Several phones and smart devices are active
  5. Someone is uploading files to cloud storage

A higher-speed connection gives these activities more room to coexist without one large transfer consuming most of the available downstream capacity.

This is different from saying that a single Netflix stream “needs” 500Mbps.

It does not.

The value of 500Mbps appears when many activities overlap.

Address-Level Reality

Unifi's coverage checker explicitly asks users to check their installation address and confirms that port availability needs to be checked before proceeding.

That distinction matters for Malaysian high-rise properties.

A building can have Unifi service while an individual unit still encounters an installation limitation because of available ports or the condition of the building's internal infrastructure.

Older properties may also involve legacy copper-based access arrangements rather than a straightforward FTTH installation.

If the physical access network cannot support the intended service, upgrading the router will not solve the problem.

For a 500Mbps subscription, confirm that your specific unit can receive the advertised service over the appropriate fibre infrastructure before considering the package price.

You may be paying for unused capacity if:

  1. Only 1–3 people regularly use the connection
  2. Most usage is ordinary browsing and streaming
  3. Your main issue is weak Wi-Fi coverage
  4. Your devices are limited to older Wi-Fi standards
  5. Your Ethernet hardware is still 100Mbps
  6. You rarely perform large downloads or uploads

If your actual problem is poor signal in a bedroom or upstairs area, moving from 300Mbps to 500Mbps will not automatically fix it.

NetBijak’s Recommendation

The 500Mbps PrimePromo makes sense when your household is consistently hitting the limits of a lower-speed connection through simultaneous usage.

At RM149/month, with three months of broadband charges waived, the promotional subscription calculation comes to RM3,576 across 24 paid months, or RM132.44/month when the cost is spread over the 27-month commitment.

The more meaningful upgrade is not simply the extra 200Mbps download capacity. The plan also moves to 100Mbps upload, which gives it a stronger case for households that regularly move large amounts of data in both directions.

For a home with 6–8 active users, that additional headroom can be useful. For a smaller household, the extra RM20/month over the 300Mbps promotional price may not produce a meaningful real-world difference unless you actually have heavy simultaneous usage.

Before applying, verify the exact promotional price period, the total charges after the promotional period, your address-level port availability, and whether your wired and wireless equipment can negotiate at speeds high enough to use the connection.

500Mbps is worth paying for when concurrency and large data transfers are the problem. If neither is an issue, 300Mbps may deliver most of the practical benefit for less.
